A learning guide · On-policy distillation for LLMs

Learning from your own mistakes, one token at a time

On-policy distillation lets a student model write its own answers while a teacher marks every token. Follow the idea from its roots in distillation and imitation learning to the 2026 research wave, and learn to read its papers in a sensible order.

The story starts before large language models, with two older ideas about learning from a teacher.

Part I · Roots, 2010–2016

Before OPD: teachers, imitators and drift

Distillation taught small models from big ones, while sequence models and imitation learning found the flaw in learning only from someone else’s path.

An everyday picture

A learner driver who only watches the instructor’s laps never practices recovering from a drift toward the curb. One who drives while the instructor comments on the route actually taken does.

Soft targets

The teacher is the model that supervises; the student is the one being trained. A language model’s raw scores for every vocabulary token are its logits, and a softmax turns them into the next-token distribution, the probabilities for the next token given the text so far.

Knowledge distillation trains the student to match soft targets, the teacher’s whole distribution. In the running example (an illustration), after … = 340 + 68 =, a teacher might put most probability on 408 and a little on near misses like 418, showing which mistakes are close.

IntuitionA distribution says how sure the teacher is and what else it considered. One correct token says neither.

Neither the 2015 recipe nor sequence-level knowledge distillation (2016), which trains on teacher-written sequences, let the student choose its inputs.

Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network Mar 2015
Hinton, Vinyals and Dean raise the softmax temperature on a fixed transfer set so the relative probabilities of wrong answers carry signal. Experiments cover only classification and speech.
Sequence-Level Knowledge Distillation Jun 2016
Kim and Rush train a translation student on its teacher’s beam-search outputs, generated before training, so the student practices on the teacher’s path, never its own.

Teacher forcing and drift

A prefix, or state, is the prompt plus the tokens generated so far. Teacher forcing trains on correct prefixes from the data, but at generation the model reads its own tokens. Once the student writes 418 (an illustration), every later token is predicted from a prefix no training example contained.

A model trained only on data prefixes never practices recovering from its own slips, a flaw called exposure bias. More broadly, training and use draw prefixes from different distributions, a distribution shift, so one slip can invite the next.

IntuitionThe student’s own mistakes are the part of the curriculum nobody wrote down.

Two 2015 papers put the model on its own path, still without a teacher.

Scheduled Sampling Jun 2015
Bengio et al. feed the model its own previous token at a growing share of positions, while targets stay ground truth and no teacher is involved.
MIXER Nov 2015
Ranzato et al. train ever-longer sentence endings on the model’s own samples, rewarded by BLEU or ROUGE. They wrote, “We refer to this discrepancy as exposure bias.”

Imitation learning’s answer

Imitation learning, where a learner copies an expert’s demonstrations, met this flaw earlier. Its simplest form, often called behavior cloning, trains only on states the expert visited. DAgger lets the learner act, has the expert label the states reached, aggregates them and retrains. Under the paper’s assumptions, errors compound roughly quadratically with task length for pure imitation and roughly linearly for DAgger.

Watch outDAgger’s guarantees cover sequential imitation under its own assumptions, not token-level distillation of language models.
DAgger Nov 2010
Ross, Gordon and Bagnell label states visited by the learner, or a learner-expert mixture, with expert actions and retrain on everything. Tested on games and handwriting recognition.

Naming the two regimes

A language model acts as a policy, choosing each next token. Training on teacher-written text, reference text or an earlier student’s answers is off-policy, since another policy produced its states. Training at the prefixes of the current student’s own samples is on-policy. The figure below sets the two regimes side by side.

Key idea

A student trained only on someone else’s path never practices recovering from its own mistakes. On-policy learning puts the student’s own states into the training set.

Check yourself: why does teacher forcing create a train-test mismatch?

Training always feeds correct prefixes; generation feeds the model’s own outputs.

Check yourself: what does DAgger collect that plain imitation never sees?

Expert labels at states the learner itself reached, aggregated over rounds.

Check yourself: is sequence-level distillation on-policy?

No. The teacher generated its targets, so the student never trains on its own samples.

In June 2023, two papers carried this lesson into large language models, letting the student write and the teacher mark what it wrote.

Part II · The first recipes, 2023

2023: the teacher reads the student’s own answers

Two papers moved the teacher’s marks onto text the student wrote itself, and asked which way to measure the gap.

The imitation shortcut

In 2023, an emerging shortcut for improving a weaker model was to fine-tune it on outputs collected from a stronger one. This is supervised fine-tuning (SFT), off-policy next-token training on fixed target text.

The False Promise of Imitating Proprietary LLMs May 2023
Fine-tuned 1.5B–13B models on up to 150M tokens of ChatGPT outputs. Crowd raters found the imitators competitive with ChatGPT; targeted benchmarks showed limited gains where imitation data was thin.
IntuitionCopied answers teach a teacher’s style faster than its substance, and raters can miss the gap.

The loop

Imitating teacher text leaves the student’s own slips unpracticed, Part I’s problem again. MiniLLM was first posted on 14 June 2023, nine days before GKD. Both train a student on its own samples while a teacher marks every token, the idea now called on-policy distillation (OPD). ImitKD (2020) had applied DAgger’s idea to distillation before; GKD treats it as a mixed-data special case. One step:

  1. The current student samples a response, called a rollout.
  2. The teacher reads each prefix of it and returns its next-token distribution.
  3. A divergence, a measure of how much two distributions disagree, compares teacher and student at every prefix.
  4. The student is updated, and the loop samples again.

In the toy answer from the opening (an illustration), at the prefix ending 340 + 68 = the teacher favors 408, so its strongest mark falls on the student’s 418. Most recipes here start from a student already fine-tuned on fixed data; Rethinking OPD rescued failing runs with such a cold start.

1

GKD

On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes

Agarwal et al. Jun 2023

The question What if the teacher graded the student’s own samples?

A coin flip with probability λ picks, batch by batch, fresh student outputs or fixed-dataset examples. At every prefix the teacher supplies its full next-token distribution, and the student minimizes a chosen divergence there, with no gradient through the sampling.

Read it for the boxed loss alongside Algorithm 1, its sampling procedure.

Watch out only the student-sampled share is on-policy. The best divergence varied by task and decoding temperature. With T5 students, headline runs used forward KL for summarization (XSum) and arithmetic reasoning (GSM8K) and a generalized JSD near forward KL for translation (WMT); reverse KL did best in instruction tuning.

Which way to measure the gap

An everyday picture

A teacher knows two ways to compute 17 × 24: split 24 into 20 + 4, or 17 into 10 + 7. A student with room for one can blur both or commit to one.

In the widget, press “Fit forward KL”, then “Fit reverse KL”.

Forward KL charges for neglecting anything the teacher supports, so its fit spreads over both humps and peaks in the nearly empty valley. Reverse KL charges for student mass where the teacher has little, so its fit takes one hump. The pulls are named mode-covering and mode-seeking, respectively.

The contrast needs a student that cannot copy the teacher; with free logits at one prefix, both divergences reach zero at an exact copy. AKL (2024) argues they differ in what they fit first, forward KL the head and reverse KL the tail. In real students, capacity, training and the estimator shape each pull.

At a prefix s=(x,y<t)s=(x,y_{<t}), with student πθ(a∣s)\pi_\theta(a\mid s) and teacher q(a∣sT)q(a\mid s^T), where sTs^T is the teacher’s view (here the same prefix):

DFKL(s)=∑a∈Vq(a∣sT) log⁡q(a∣sT)πθ(a∣s)DRKL(s)=∑a∈Vπθ(a∣s) log⁡πθ(a∣s)q(a∣sT)\begin{aligned} D_{\mathrm{FKL}}(s) &= \sum_{a\in V} q(a\mid s^T)\,\log\frac{q(a\mid s^T)}{\pi_\theta(a\mid s)} \\ D_{\mathrm{RKL}}(s) &= \sum_{a\in V} \pi_\theta(a\mid s)\,\log\frac{\pi_\theta(a\mid s)}{q(a\mid s^T)} \end{aligned}

Forward KL weights each log-ratio by the teacher’s probability, reverse KL by the student’s; the sum over the vocabulary VV compares alternatives at one prefix.

GKD’s middle path, the generalized Jensen-Shannon divergence (generalized JSD), compares each distribution with their blend; a weight β\beta slides it between the two KLs.

Key idea

OPD separates two choices: whose states you train on, and how you compare the two distributions at each state.

Reverse KL over whole responses

GKD treats each sampled prefix as fixed data, with no gradient through the sampling. But one early token changes every later prefix, so a whole-response objective needs credit assignment, deciding which earlier tokens caused later discrepancies. MiniLLM does this for reverse KL; Part III shows its gradient.

2

MiniLLM

MiniLLM: On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models

Gu et al. Jun 2023

The question Can a student minimize reverse KL over whole responses?

Each sampled token’s reward is the teacher-to-student log-probability ratio, and a policy-gradient update credits each token with the rewards that follow it. In practice the gradient splits into GKD’s exact per-position reverse KL plus a sampled future-return term. Each token is drawn from a 20% teacher, 80% student blend, so prefixes are partly teacher-written.

Read it for how future returns credit earlier tokens for later discrepancies.

Watch out its evidence is instruction following and sets no rule that reverse KL is best for reasoning. Without its stabilizers, students learned “repeated, short, or meaningless strings” the teacher found likely; removing length normalization cut validation Rouge-L from 27.4 to 17.4 (GPT-2 1.5B to 125M).

Forward KL, about 1.27 nats against 0.60 (illustrative, computed), because near-zero student mass on a teacher-supported token is costly.

Part II · The first recipes, 2024

2024: cheaper, mixed and at scale

Researchers reused student outputs to save cost, mixed teacher and student tokens, and distilled at pretraining scale on fixed text.

The bill for one step

One on-policy step pays three bills: generating rollouts, teacher scoring (running the teacher on student prefixes) and the update. Reuse trims the first bill but leaves stale rollouts, samples from an earlier student. DistiLLM combines reuse with a skewed KL, a KL against a teacher-student blend, which makes its gradients more stable.

3

DistiLLM

DistiLLM: Towards Streamlined Distillation for Large Language Models

Ko et al. Feb 2024

The question Can student outputs be reused to save collection cost?

Training starts on fixed data, since teacher feedback on a weak student’s unfamiliar outputs can mislead, and raises the share of student outputs whenever validation loss rises. New outputs enter a replay buffer that batches draw from repeatedly. Its main runs use skewed reverse KL with mixing weight 0.1.

Read it for how loss design ties into the cost and freshness of training data.

Watch out buffered outputs come from earlier checkpoints, so training is partly off-policy. The 2.5–4.3× speedup over MiniLLM and GKD comes from its instruction-following runs; it has no math benchmark.

Co-written states

MiniLLM already mixed in teacher tokens at random. In October 2024, SKD made the mix selective. In the toy answer (an illustration), if the teacher ranks the drafted 418 poorly, the teacher’s own sample, perhaps 408, replaces it.

Speculative Knowledge Distillation (SKD) Oct 2024
Keeps a drafted student token only if it ranks in the teacher’s top 25 (an untuned setting), otherwise samples from the teacher; training matches distributions on these teacher-corrected sequences.
IntuitionStates can be co-written; the teacher rewrites more drafted tokens early on and fewer as the student improves.

Distillation at pretraining scale

In mid-2024, Gemma 2 made a larger model’s soft targets the main pretraining signal for its smaller models.

Gemma 2

Gemma 2: Improving Open Language Models at a Practical Size

Gemma Team Jul 2024

The question What does distillation at pretraining scale, on fixed text, buy?

The 2B and 9B models learn the teacher’s next-token distribution on training text instead of the one-hot next token, over 2T and 8T tokens, more than 50 times the compute-optimal amount. A small ablation (2B student, 500B tokens, 7B teacher) averaged 67.7 distilled against 60.3 from scratch over three benchmarks.

Part III · The industrial turn, 2025

2025: reasoning models and the place OPD found

Reinforcement learning became central to reasoning models, and OPD found its niche as an on-policy method with a mark at every token.

Reasoning through RL, small models through traces

The year opened with a large-scale use of the off-policy route. DeepSeek-R1 built its own reasoning mainly with reinforcement learning, then passed it to smaller models by fine-tuning them on its outputs. In RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR), an automatic checker scores each sampled answer, say whether a math result is right, so the model gets a sparse reward, one number per response. A policy gradient update makes high-scoring samples more likely and low-scoring ones less likely. GRPO, OPD’s usual RL partner or baseline, scores each answer against others sampled for the same prompt.

IntuitionA sparse reward says the answer is wrong. It never says which step went wrong.
19

DeepSeek-R1

DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

DeepSeek-AI et al. Jan 2025

The question How far can small models get from a strong reasoner’s curated outputs alone?

Six open base models, from Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B to Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, got supervised fine-tuning on about 800,000 curated samples generated with R1 (about 200k of them non-reasoning data from the DeepSeek-V3 pipeline). No RL followed. On AIME 2024 the distilled 32B scored 72.6 pass@1, against 47.0 for the same base model after large-scale RL alone.

Read it for a large-scale teacher-trace baseline an on-policy stage must beat.

Watch out the students never train on their own rollouts, so this is an off-policy comparator; its RL comparison uses one base model and one recipe.

Because a teacher only approximates the truth, a student trained repeatedly on fixed teacher data can grow closer to its teacher while getting worse at the task, an effect called teacher hacking, which fresh samples mitigated in the study below. Part IV weighs their cost.

On Teacher Hacking Feb 2025
An oracle model stands in for the truth, so distance to the teacher and to the truth can be measured separately. In T5 experiments, the hacking came from many epochs on fixed data.

A model family puts OPD in its pipeline

Qwen3

Qwen3 Technical Report

Yang et al. May 2025

The question Where does OPD sit in a production pipeline for small models?

Six smaller models are fine-tuned on teacher outputs, then on their own samples with logits aligned to a teacher’s (Qwen3-32B or Qwen3-235B-A22B) to minimize a KL divergence. From one off-policy distilled Qwen3-8B checkpoint, on math and code queries, OPD reaches 74.4 on AIME’24 in 1,800 GPU hours and RL 67.6 in 17,920 (Table 21).

Read it for OPD as one stage of a pipeline, and Table 21’s pass@64 on AIME’24, which OPD raised from 90.0 to 93.3 (one problem of 30) while RL did not.

Watch out the GPU hours exclude preparing the teacher, and the Table 21 teacher and the KL direction go unstated.

In June, KDRL added to GRPO a separate reverse-KL loss toward the teacher at sampled tokens; folding that KL into the reward instead collapsed training.

Dense meets RL

The figure sorts methods by where their states come from and how dense their feedback is. SFT and classic distillation imitate fixed text token by token; filtered imitation keeps or drops whole answers, as R1’s rejection-sampled data did; RLVR scores the student’s answers. OPD pairs the student’s own samples with dense supervision, a signal at every token.

Key idea

OPD sits between RL and classic distillation. Its states come from the student, as in RL, and its feedback arrives at every token, as in distillation.

A Thinking Machines Lab post framed OPD this way in October 2025.

Thinking Machines Lab: On-Policy Distillation Oct 2025
Turns the teacher-student log-ratio on each sampled token into per-token feedback for a policy-gradient update. Each token gets credit only for its own log-ratio, with no full-vocabulary distributions or future returns.

That log-ratio, taken on the token the student actually sampled, is the sampled-token signal, like the marks in the opening widget. Averaged over the student’s possible samples, it equals minus the reverse KL at that prefix, so the post calls its loss per-token reverse KL. Full-vocabulary supervision instead computes the whole sum, which needs every teacher logit.

AsideOne number per token is cheap, yet teacher-preferred tokens the student rarely samples get almost no direct correction.
For the mathematically curious: an objective is not its surrogate

Part IV · Dial 3 · Policy update

How a mark becomes an update

How much of the teacher an update reads, and how, shapes the method as much as the divergence.

Three widths of signal

The third dial decides how much of the teacher’s distribution the update reads at each token, and how that signal becomes a gradient. Picture three chess coaches. One judges only your move, one names the best two, one rates every move.

OPD has the same three widths: the sampled-token signal, top-K supervision over a few top tokens ranked by the teacher or the student (often rescaled to sum to one), and full-vocabulary supervision.

When the sampled signal misleads

Revisiting OPD traced the sampled signal’s brittleness to signal imbalance (Dial 3), unreliable teacher guidance (Dial 2) and tokenizer or special-token mismatch, an interface problem where the models split text or mark special tokens differently. Rethinking OPD, in contrast, found sampled-token rewards comparable to its top-k settings.

Revisiting OPD

Revisiting On-Policy Distillation: Empirical Failure Modes and Simple Fixes

Fu et al. Mar 2026

The question Why does sampled-token OPD turn brittle on long rollouts?

Its sampled-token reward penalizes most tokens, lets a few positive ones dominate, and can miss repetition loops. The repair matches renormalized distributions over the teacher’s top 32 tokens, samples with top-p 0.9 and masks problematic special tokens.

Read it for the ablations. In single-task math (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct student, OpenThinker3-7B teacher), the five-benchmark average was 36.4 at baseline, 40.7 with masking alone and 41.5 fully repaired.

Watch out top-K matching without top-p fell below baseline on AIME24 avg@32 (17.7 against 20.4; 23.6 with top-p), and the repaired student still trails its teacher (41.5 against 56.0).

EOPD

Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation of Language Models

Jin et al. Mar 2026

The question What should the update do where the teacher is unsure?

Reverse KL tends to narrow the student onto a few of a hesitant teacher’s plausible tokens. EOPD keeps its clipped, sampled-token reverse-KL term everywhere and, where teacher entropy exceeds 0.8, adds forward KL over the teacher’s renormalized top 16 tokens.

Read it for an additive design with both terms at one position.

Watch out it never turns reverse KL off. Validation centers on math; forward KL at every position was competitive (1.7B student) but used about 7.4 times as much extra memory.

Mixing teacher and outcome

EOPD’s added term matches distributions directly. Other updates turn teacher scores into returns or advantages (scores measured against a baseline) for a policy gradient; each needs its own definition.

Policy-gradient updates where the teacher favors the sampled token more than the student does, teacher-top-K forward KL elsewhere, so the update and the tokens it reads change together.
G-OPD Feb 2026
Rewards the teacher-to-reference log-ratio with a KL penalty to the reference; scaling the reward past 1 extrapolates the target. Students beat teachers only in some tested settings.

Uni-OPD

Uni-OPD: Unifying On-Policy Distillation with a Dual-Perspective Recipe

Hou et al. May 2026

The question Can outcome correctness calibrate the teacher’s token signal?

Uni-OPD averages each rollout’s sampled-token log-ratios into one teacher score. On math (Qwen3-4B student, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 teacher), that score ranked about 45% of comparable correct-incorrect pairs the wrong way, close to a coin flip. It shifts or masks scores until correct rollouts lead and gives all of a rollout’s tokens that one advantage, so the teacher’s within-rollout credit goes unused; it also balances difficulty and correctness.

Read it for the runs with calibration alone, which show what the outcome check adds to the teacher signal.

Watch out balancing and calibration act together in the tested pipeline, and calibration needs a verifier and groups mixing right and wrong answers.

The whole vocabulary without paying full price

Plain sampled-token OPD already gives an unbiased estimator of the full-vocabulary reverse-KL gradient at a fixed prefix, but a noisy one; in SparseOPD’s 4B math test its gradient had cosine 0.29 with the full gradient, against 0.90 for top-K. Two papers cut that noise while staying unbiased, which renormalized top-K is not.

TT-OPD Sep 2026
Exact terms for a selected set, by default the student’s top 16 tokens, plus a sampled tail correction; unbiased conditional on a fixed prefix.
ResOPD Oct 2026
Lumps the unseen tail into one bucket of known mass, sampling only the residual. Unbiased when rollouts sample the full distribution; its own main runs use top-p and top-k sampling, outside that proof.
SparseOPD Sep 2026
Reads the full vocabulary, then backpropagates through a few logits. Versus full-vocabulary training (4B, 8K tokens), peak extra backward memory fell 70.5% and the complete update took 14.5% longer.

Part V · The frontier · Interaction

When the student acts: tools, memory and teams

In agents all three dials move at once, and the teacher has to judge exactly the state the student was in.

The position on the board

When a student calls a tool, part of its context is written by the world instead of by the student.

An everyday picture

A chess coach reviewing your game sets up the exact position you reached, opponent’s moves included, and grades only your moves.

Recast the running example as an agent (an illustration): the student writes calc(340 + 68) and a calculator returns 408. All three dials move: the state holds tool output and memory, and the loss weight is zero on the tool’s 408 (Dial 1); the teacher must read exactly that state (Dial 2); and the update mixes teacher marks with outcome RL, as both papers below do (Dial 3). Keeping tool output in context but out of the loss is called observation masking.

Tools and memory

SOD

SOD: Step-wise On-policy Distillation for Small Language Model Agents

Zhong et al. May 2026

The question After a bad tool call, should the student still trust the teacher?

SOD splits a Python-tool trajectory into steps at tool outputs and scores each by the mean absolute student-teacher log-probability gap on the student’s tokens. When a step diverges more than the first did, as after a bad tool call, its distillation weight falls; as the gap shrinks, the weight rises again, up to 1.2. GRPO runs alongside; tool outputs never enter the loss.

Read it for the ablations: among weighting schemes, zeroing distillation after the first tool error did worst (31.85% average against 42.98%, 1.7B student).

Watch out the score only approximates KL, its premise (drift means unreliable guidance) rests on teacher-entropy statistics and ablations, and all runs use Qwen3 models with one Python interpreter.

SPICE Sep 2026
From MLToolBench: a frozen policy copy rereads each tool call with and without verified solution summaries, and the log-ratio becomes a turn reward in outcome RL. Its update is not an exact conditional-KL estimator.

On the tracks SOD, MemOPD and Revisiting DAgger make up the whole track How does interaction change supervision?

The environment wrote them, so they hold no student decision; later tokens still depend on them.

Check yourself: why not score a flattened history?

Its marks would belong to a state the student never visited.

The training histories, which include teacher turns the student lacks when it runs alone.

Part V · The frontier · Limits

What improves, what degrades

Diagnostic studies show what a higher score can hide: a teacher that does not fit, borrowed confidence, narrower coverage and abilities that move the other way.

Beyond one number

pass@k counts a problem solved if any of k sampled answers is correct, so pass@1 checks a typical answer and a large k measures coverage. A calibrated model is right about as often as it claims. Retention means earlier abilities survive training. The benchmarks guide adds length, termination and costs by stage.

Will the teacher’s reasoning fit?

The reading order’s short introduction ends here, after GKD and MiniLLM.

Rethinking OPD

Rethinking On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models: Phenomenology, Mechanism, and Recipe

Li et al. Apr 2026

The question When does a teacher’s reasoning transfer to a student?

In one math comparison, the teacher with lower benchmark scores taught a 1.7B student better. Teachers helped when their thinking patterns were compatible with the student’s and they knew something new; a larger model from the student’s own training pipeline added little. The paper’s lens is the overlap of both models’ top-16 next-token candidates on student rollouts; in these runs shared tokens carry 97–99% of both models’ probability mass, and training on them alone kept nearly the full benefit.

Read it for teacher preparation, cold start and prompt template as controlled variables.

Watch out its main experiments use 1.5B–1.7B students on math, and top-k overlap is a diagnostic there, not a universal necessary-and-sufficient test.

Watch outOverlap can mislead; in the paper’s own runs, its curves converge while a performance gap persists.

Confidence and diversity

CaOPD is a transfer station between two reading tracks: the self-teacher track ends here and the degradation track passes through.

CaOPD

The Illusion of Certainty: Decoupling Capability and Calibration in On-Policy Distillation

Zhang et al. Apr 2026

The question Whose information should justify the student’s confidence?

A self-teacher that has seen a solution sounds sure, and the student copies the certainty without the information; in the paper’s runs, self-distillation with a demonstration in the teacher’s context (SDFT) or a verified rollout (SDPO) worsened overconfidence. CaOPD measures how often the student itself succeeds over K samples (K = 8 recommended) and writes that rate as the stated confidence, in the target and in the teacher’s context.

Read it for calibration beside accuracy; on Qwen3-8B Tool Use the overconfidence gap fell from +32.0% (SDFT) to −0.7%.

Watch out it needs parseable confidence, a verifier and extra rollouts, and was tested on Science Q&A and Tool Use only.

IntuitionA teacher that saw the answer has earned its confidence; the student, who never sees it, has not.
17

Sampled-demonstration diversity study

On-Policy Self-Distillation with Sampled Demonstrations Reduces Output Diversity

Nicolicioiu et al. Jun 2026

The question Does self-distillation with sampled demonstrations narrow the solutions a model finds?

The SDPO-style self-teacher sees randomly drawn correct rollouts and favors student rollouts that resemble them. In its theory, training tilts probability toward solutions that already dominate, while KL-regularized RL keeps the ratios among equally correct answers. Empirically pass@1 stays competitive and pass@16 falls (Qwen3-8B, average SciKnowEval pass@16: 83.6 with GRPO, 78.5–78.7 with self-distillation).

Read it for its pass@k curves, and token entropy failing to track diversity.

Watch out the theory assumes a frozen base teacher, the experiments an exponential-moving-average (EMA) teacher; this is no theorem about every on-policy method.

Where the updates land

Sparse updates are easy to read as forgetting less. Two studies measured where OPD’s updates land.

16

On the Geometry of OPD

On the Geometry of On-Policy Distillation

Shen et al. Jun 2026

The question Where in parameter space do OPD updates go?

In Qwen3 runs (8B student, 32B teacher, math), OPD left 51.6% of weights unchanged at bf16 precision, between SFT’s 8.1% and RLVR’s 77.2%. OPD’s 16 main directions align with the final ones from the first measured checkpoint. Confining later gradients to the directions found at about 20% of training left AIME 2024 avg@8 essentially unchanged; it hurt SFT.

Read it for how concentration develops over training.

Watch out the paradigms differ in starting point, data and steps, so the ordering describes these runs; a shared warning follows.

Dense Supervision, Sparse Updates

Dense Supervision, Sparse Updates: On the Sparsity and Geometry of On-Policy Distillation

Yu et al. Jun 2026

The question How sparse are OPD updates, and where?

In its OPD checkpoints, updates leave most individual weights visibly unchanged, yet are spread across layers and modules. Update matrices are numerically full-rank, so every direction moves a little, yet a few directions carry much of the change.

Read it for masked retraining: moving only the coordinates a finished OPD run changed nearly matched full training (peak 35.10% against 35.52%, R1-Qwen on AIME 2024/25). It pairs with the geometry card.

Watch out for both item-16 cards: concentration, sparsity and numerical rank are different properties, and neither study shows that concentrated updates preserve capabilities.

AsideThe identity matrix is sparse yet full-rank; a dense outer product has rank one.

Skills, facts and what recovery hides

With Semi-OPD (Dial 1), the studies below ask what OPD transfers. Reverse KL barely penalizes a student for missing an answer it gives almost no probability, and an unknown fact is such an answer.

On-Policy Distillation Teaches New Skills but Not New Knowledge Oct 2026
Crosses prefix source with KL direction on lookup-table tasks (one seed). Student prefixes helped skills under either direction; reverse KL transferred held-out facts far less reliably, and forward KL substantially improved factual transfer with either prefix source.
ReCal Oct 2026
Reweights the pruning-calibration tokens that decide which channels are cut; recovery (SFT, then OPD) is unchanged. Against standard pruning calibration, AIME improved in seven of eight settings and IFBench (instruction following) declined in all eight.

Reading critically

Myths and evidence

Eight tempting slogans, each tested against what the cited papers report.

Each claim below sounds reasonable, and each meets a source in this guide that complicates it. Each evidence line restates a boundary from the linked cards; before reading it, name the condition the slogan drops, such as the task, the budget or what was measured.

Reverse KL is the right objective for OPD.

GKD found task-dependent preferences among divergences, and MiniLLM’s main evaluation is instruction following. In controlled lookup-table tasks, forward KL substantially improved factual transfer over reverse KL (Skills vs. Knowledge).

On-policy means fresh samples at every step, and fresher always wins.

DistiLLM reuses student outputs and Lightning-OPD caches teacher scores. In 14 of 17 math teacher-student pairs, rollouts sampled once from the initial student beat continual refresh (Semi-OPD), yet in a many-epoch T5 study fresh generations reduced teacher hacking.

Self-distillation gives you supervision for free.

OPSD needs a useful reference solution, SDPO needs feedback the model can use, and SD-Zero pays for a binary verifier, verified revisions and reviser training. Shared weights do not close the information gap.

Dense teacher feedback beats RL across the board.

OPSD’s v3 wins over GRPO compare selected checkpoints under different budgets and shrink with scale (43.4 versus 37.7 at 1.7B, 64.8 versus 64.0 at 8B). SDPO trailed GRPO on Qwen2.5-1.5B, and the diversity study pairs competitive pass@1 with lower pass@16.

Supervising half the tokens halves the cost.

TIP reports peak-memory savings of up to 47% in its half-token math setting. A loss mask leaves rollout generation and teacher scoring untouched; the cost ledger shows where each stage sits.

When the teacher disagrees, the student is wrong.

Disagreement shows only that the teacher prefers something else; TIP does not check that the teacher’s preferred continuation is correct. SCOPE’s perplexity-recovery link is a correlation from one math setting.

If the target score rises, nothing else was lost.

Against standard pruning calibration, ReCal raised AIME in seven of eight settings while IFBench fell in all eight, after the same SFT and OPD recovery. CaOPD’s self-distilled students inherited confidence only the teacher’s privileged information justified, and neither item-16 study (geometry, sparse updates) shows concentrated updates preserve capabilities.

A strong model trained with OPD proves that the OPD stage paid off.

Qwen3’s and DeepSeek-V4’s overall scores are not isolated OPD effects. In the consolidation study, the multi-teacher fusion stage cost under one-fifth of training the per-domain experts, but counting them, the total reached 1.14 to 1.19 times their training cost alone.

Reference

Glossary

Forty-five terms in plain words.

The terms are grouped by the part of the story that explains them, in the order it explains them.

Part I · Roots

Teacher
The supervising model, often stronger, sometimes the student itself given extra context.
Student
The model being trained; in OPD it writes the responses the teacher marks.
Logits
Raw scores a model gives each token before softmax turns them into probabilities.
Next-token distribution
The probabilities a model gives every possible next token, given the current prefix.
Knowledge distillation
Training a student to match a teacher’s outputs, usually to compress a large model.
Soft targets
The teacher’s full, often temperature-softened distribution, including small probabilities on wrong answers.
Sequence-level knowledge distillation
Fine-tuning the student on complete answers the teacher wrote before training began.
Prefix or state
The prompt plus the tokens generated so far, where the next prediction starts.
Teacher forcing
Training with the correct previous tokens always fed in, never the model’s own outputs.
Exposure bias
The mismatch when a model trained on correct prefixes must continue from its own outputs.
Distribution shift
Training and deployment draw prefixes from different distributions, leaving some states unpracticed.
Imitation learning
Learning to act from expert demonstrations; behavior cloning copies expert actions in expert states.
DAgger (Dataset Aggregation)
The learner acts, an expert labels each visited state, and labels accumulate over rounds.
Off-policy
Training on states produced by others: reference text, the teacher or an earlier student.
On-policy
Training on states sampled from the current student; reuse, delays or top-p sampling make practice approximate.

Part II · The first recipes

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT)
Ordinary fine-tuning on fixed target text, written by people or by a teacher.
On-policy distillation (OPD)
The student writes its own responses, and a teacher gives feedback on them token by token.
Rollout
A response the student samples; its prefixes are the states that receive feedback.
Forward KL
A teacher-weighted gap, DKL(q∥πθ)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\parallel\pi_\theta), penalizing the student for neglecting tokens the teacher finds plausible.
Reverse KL
A student-weighted gap, DKL(πθ∥q)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\parallel q), penalizing student probability where the teacher puts little.
Mode-seeking and mode-covering
If the student cannot match the teacher, reverse KL tends toward one peak, forward KL toward all.
Generalized Jensen-Shannon divergence (generalized JSD)
A blend of forward and reverse KL against a teacher-student mixture, weighted by β\beta.
Credit assignment
Deciding which earlier tokens deserve credit or blame for what happens later.
Teacher scoring
Running the teacher on student prefixes for its probabilities, a cost separate from generation.
Stale rollouts
Responses from an earlier student version, reused after the student has changed.
Skewed KL
KL measured against a teacher-student blend, so tiny probabilities cannot blow up the loss.

Part III · The industrial turn

RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR)
Reinforcement learning rewarded by an automatic checker, such as a math grader or tests.
Sparse (outcome) reward
One score per response, usually right or wrong, silent on which tokens mattered.
Policy gradient
Making sampled outputs more or less likely in proportion to how well they scored.
GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)
An RL method scoring each sampled response against others drawn for the same prompt.
Dense supervision
A learning signal at every token, instead of one score for the whole response.
Sampled-token signal
The teacher-student log-probability gap on the token the student actually sampled, one number per position.
Full-vocabulary supervision
Comparing teacher and student on every vocabulary token at each position, including unsampled ones.

Part IV · The 2026 wave

Multi-teacher distillation
Training one student from several specialists, choosing or combining their targets at each state.
Behavior policy
The model μ\mu whose samples supply the training states; on-policy, it is the current student.
Token entropy
How unsure a model is about its next token; high entropy means many plausible options.
Self-distillation
The model, or an earlier copy of it, teaches itself, usually seeing extra context the student lacks.
Privileged information
Context the teacher sees in training that the deployed student lacks, such as a solution.
Top-K supervision
Comparing only K top tokens, ranked by teacher or student, often renormalized over that set.
Tokenizer or special-token mismatch
Teacher and student split text or use control tokens differently, so probabilities describe different events.
Unbiased estimator
A noisy estimate that is right on average, under the conditions its proof assumes.

Part V · The frontier

Observation masking
Leaving tool or environment output out of the loss while keeping it in context.
pass@k
The chance that any of k attempts succeeds; pass@1 scores a single attempt.
Calibration
Confidence matching accuracy, so answers given 80% confidence are right about 80% of the time.
Retention
Keeping abilities that training did not target, checked against the starting checkpoint.
